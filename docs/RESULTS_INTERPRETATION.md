# Interpretation and limitations

## Supported conclusions

1. Marker-supported cDC1 cells are detectable across multiple bladder-tumor patients.
2. Patient-level pseudobulk data confirm a cDC1 identity program and increased expression of selected cross-presentation genes, especially `TAP1` and `WDFY4`.
3. The locked cross-presentation score is associated with an inflamed tumor microenvironment and favorable immunotherapy outcomes in unadjusted analyses.
4. Much of the clinical association overlaps with TMB, cDC1 abundance and T-cell inflammation.

## Conclusions not supported by the current data

- that the complete MHC-I cross-presentation pathway is uniformly activated in every cDC1 cell;
- that the score is an independent or clinically deployable predictive biomarker;
- that transcriptomic association proves antigen presentation or T-cell priming experimentally;
- that UMAP proximity establishes lineage or causal transition;
- that all significant DE genes are independent discoveries, because XCR1/CLEC9A/WDFY4 also contribute to cell identity definitions.

## Main limitations

- only eight patients meet the primary paired pseudobulk cell-count criteria;
- cDC1 is rare and sensitive to sequencing depth and QC choices;
- bulk immunotherapy scores cannot separate abundance from per-cell activation completely;
- no independent spatial or functional validation is included yet;
- adjusted clinical models show attenuation, arguing against strong standalone predictive claims.

## Recommended extensions

- external single-cell and spatial-transcriptomic validation;
- multiplex immunofluorescence for cDC1, CD8 T cells and spatial niches;
- antigen uptake/cross-presentation and T-cell activation assays;
- prospective validation of the locked score without outcome-driven retuning.
